🧠 Second Brain — University Vault

A personal knowledge base built in Obsidian, organized by course and wired together into a connected graph rather than a pile of isolated notes.

This vault isn't just a notes folder — it's an actively linked knowledge graph. Every course is cross-referenced against every other course, connections are surfaced (not just filed away), and an AI research assistant helps keep it that way.

✨ What makes this different
Cross-course connections — Concepts, theories, and readings are linked across course folders, not siloed within them. A framework from one course that reframes an argument in another gets surfaced, not lost.
AI-assisted second brain — An AI research assistant scans the vault, proposes links, drafts research hub notes, and flags stale or disconnected material.
Visual graph — Each course is color-coded in Graph View, so the shape of my own knowledge is something I can actually see.
Course overview canvases — Every course has a Canvas board mapping lectures, assignments, and research into one visual layout, with key connections drawn out explicitly.
Spaced repetition — Key facts are tagged as flashcards and reviewed on a schedule, not crammed the night before.

🕸 How the graph works

Each course folder is assigned a distinct color in both Graph View and the file explorer, so clusters are visually identifiable at a glance. Notes are linked deliberately — the goal isn't maximum link count, it's meaningful connections: shared theories, contradicting arguments, or concepts that build on each other across courses.

🤖 Working with an AI assistant

Portions of this vault are maintained with help from an AI assistant that:

Proposes [[wikilinks]] and tags between related notes
Builds and updates course-overview canvases
Drafts research hub notes from existing material
Runs periodic reviews to catch orphaned or stale notes

All AI-proposed changes are reviewed before being applied — nothing is auto-committed without a pass through by me first.

⚠️ Note on privacy

This repo may be structured publicly, but course content itself may be excluded — check .gitignore before assuming everything here is included. Readings, assignment text, and other copyrighted or private material generally aren't meant to be redistributed.
