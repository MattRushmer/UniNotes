# 07 - Communications Management

> Auto-extracted from [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Lectures/07 - Communication Management.pptx|07 - Communication Management.pptx]]
> Source text: *Information Technology Project Management* (Schwalbe, 2019), Chapter 10

## Why communication matters
- Communication is a major PM problem: many projects struggle or fail when information isn't communicated clearly to stakeholders — goals, progress, risks, issues, expectations
- **Bull Survey** (203 IT/project managers, UK): poor communications ranked **3rd** among project failure causes (40% of respondents)
- **CHAOS Report** failure factors rooted in weak communication: lack of user input (**12.8%**) and incomplete requirements (**12.3%**)
- Complexity: communication must flow across managers, team members, users, customers, and other stakeholders

## The communication process
- **Communicator** (originator) → **message** (thoughts, feelings, ideas) → **medium** (vehicle/method) → **recipient** (who "makes or breaks" the process)
- Key roles: project manager, portfolio/program/operations/support managers, C-level executives — all must be communicators in different directions

## Communication channels
- **Formula:** number of communication channels = n(n−1)/2, where n = number of stakeholders — a combinatorics result; see [[MATH503 Mathematics/Notes/Week 04 - Permutations and Counting|MATH503 W04]]
- Simplify complexity by reducing channels — "it's all about considering your stakeholders"
- Formal vs informal methods; many people prefer informal; distribute important info effectively and timely (e.g., agile daily stand-ups)

## Communications management processes (3)
1. **Plan communications management** → communications management plan
2. **Manage communications** — getting info to the right people at the right time in a useful format
3. **Monitor communications**

## Communications management plan contents
- Stakeholder communications requirements (from the [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Notes/Week 04 - Stakeholder Management|stakeholder register]])
- Information to be communicated (format, content, level of detail) · who receives/produces it
- Methods/technologies · frequency · escalation procedures · revision procedures · glossary

## Managing communications
- Use of technology: **interactive / push / pull** methods (classify: phone, email, wiki, virtual conference…)
- **Performance reporting** keeps stakeholders informed:
  - **Progress reports** — what the team accomplished in a period
  - **Status reports** — where the project stands at a point in time
  - **Forecasts** — future status based on past info and trends

## Best practice
- **Kanban boards** for visualizing workflow
- Running effective meetings: agenda in advance, define purpose/outcome, who should attend, run professionally, set ground rules, build relationships
- Templates and dashboards for project communications
- R&D/project communications planning: identify critical stakeholders (team, client, mentor), meeting frequency, best-fit channel, tools, reporting, escalation
