# 07 - Communications Management

> Auto-extracted from [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Lectures/07 - Communication Management (full deck).pptx|07 - Communication Management (full deck).pptx]] (46-slide version; supersedes the shorter [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Lectures/07 - Communication Management.pptx|30-slide deck]] kept alongside it, same lecture)
> Source text: *Information Technology Project Management* (Schwalbe, 2019), Chapter 10

## Why communication matters
- Communication is a major PM problem: many projects struggle or fail when information isn't communicated clearly to stakeholders — goals, progress, risks, issues, expectations
- **Bull Survey** (203 IT/project managers, UK): poor communications ranked **3rd** among project failure causes (40% of respondents)
- **CHAOS Report** failure factors rooted in weak communication: lack of user input (**12.8%**) and incomplete requirements (**12.3%**)
- Complexity: communication must flow across managers, team members, users, customers, and other stakeholders

## The communication process
- **Communicator** (originator) → **message** (thoughts, feelings, ideas) → **medium** (vehicle/method) → **recipient** (who "makes or breaks" the process)
- Key roles: project manager, portfolio/program/operations/support managers, C-level executives — all must be communicators in different directions

## PMI Communication Model
- **Encode**: sender turns an idea into a message · **Transmit**: message sent through a selected medium · **Decode**: receiver interprets the message
- **Acknowledge**: receiver confirms the message was received · **Feedback/response**: receiver shows whether meaning was understood
- **Noise**: barriers that distort or block the message

## Dimensions and forms of communication
- **Dimensions**: internal vs external · formal (reports, meetings, official documents) vs informal (quick chats, email) · upward/downward/horizontal · official/unofficial, written/oral/non-verbal
- **Forms**: interpersonal (one-to-one) · small-group (team) · public (one-to-many project audience) · mass (large audience, limited interaction) · network/social (many-to-many, digital) — method and audience are separate decisions

## Effective communication: the 5 Cs
- **Correct** (accurate facts, grammar, terminology) · **Concise** (no unnecessary words) · **Clear** (obvious purpose/expected action) · **Coherent** (logical order) · **Controlled** (structured so key points are easy to follow)

## Communication channels
- **Formula:** number of communication channels = n(n−1)/2, where n = number of stakeholders — a combinatorics result; see [[MATH503 Mathematics/Notes/Week 04 - Permutations and Counting|MATH503 W04]]
- Simplify complexity by reducing channels — "it's all about considering your stakeholders"
- Formal vs informal methods; many people prefer informal; distribute important info effectively and timely (e.g., agile daily stand-ups)

## Communications management processes (3)
1. **Plan communications management** → communications management plan
2. **Manage communications** — getting info to the right people at the right time in a useful format
3. **Monitor communications**

## Communications management plan contents
- Stakeholder communications requirements (from the [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Notes/Week 04 - Stakeholder Management|stakeholder register]])
- Information to be communicated (format, content, level of detail) · who receives/produces it
- Methods/technologies · frequency · escalation procedures · revision procedures · glossary
- **Stakeholder communications analysis** (in-deck table, directly tied to Assignment 1 Part 2): for each stakeholder — communication document type, document format, contact person, due date & frequency. Explicitly stated as required for your assignment, based on your own stakeholder register

## Strategy vs plan vs communications
- **Communication strategy**: the overall approach for effective stakeholder communication
- **Communications management plan**: how communication will be planned, structured, implemented, monitored
- **Project communications**: the actual reports, presentations, minutes, updates, dashboards produced
- Main point: strategy decides the approach, the plan organises the work, communications deliver the information

## Stakeholder engagement and tailoring
- **Communication should change stakeholder engagement**: current engagement → desired engagement → the gap between them → a targeted communication response to close it (increase support, reduce resistance)
- **Tailoring communication to the stakeholder**: communication style (preferred method/format/detail) · power and influence · culture (language, hierarchy, norms) · information preference (summary vs detailed evidence) · engagement need — don't communicate with every stakeholder the same way

## Managing communications
- Use of technology: **interactive / push / pull** methods (classify: phone, email, wiki, virtual conference…) — tools rely on telecom networks (phone, texting) or the Internet (email, IM, wiki, virtual conference)
- **Performance reporting** keeps stakeholders informed:
  - **Progress reports** — what the team accomplished in a period
  - **Status reports** — where the project stands at a point in time
  - **Forecasts** — future status based on past info and trends

## Monitoring communication effectiveness
- Five checks: **right message** (did stakeholders receive what they needed?) · **right audience** (correct people?) · **right channel** (appropriate method?) · **right time** (early enough to act on?) · **right understanding** (interpreted correctly?)
- Use feedback, issue logs, surveys, dashboards, lessons learned to verify
- **Tailoring project communications** more broadly: stakeholders (detail level), location (colocated vs virtual), technology, language, knowledge management — one approach doesn't suit every project

## Communication skills and agile communication
- **Project communication skills**: active listening, feedback, non-verbal communication, presentation skills, cultural awareness, conflict/meeting management
- **Communication in agile/adaptive projects**: frequent checkpoints, visible work (boards/backlogs/dashboards), fast feedback loops, direct communication to reduce delays, adapting as requirements/priorities/risks change, keeping stakeholders close to the evolving product (not just final delivery)

## Best practice
- **Kanban boards** for visualizing workflow — people tailor the concept to their needs
- Running effective meetings: determine if it can be avoided, define purpose/outcome, decide who should attend, agenda in advance, handouts/logistics ready ahead of time, run professionally, set ground rules, build relationships
- Templates and dashboards for project communications
- R&D/project communications planning: identify critical stakeholders (team, client, mentor), meeting frequency, best-fit channel, tools, reporting, escalation
