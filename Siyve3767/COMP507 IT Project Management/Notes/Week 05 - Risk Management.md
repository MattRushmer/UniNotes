# Week 05 - Risk Management

> Auto-extracted from [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Lectures/Week 05 - Risk Management.pptx|Week 05 - Risk Management.pptx]] — Daniel Vaipulu

## What is risk?
- **Project risk** = an uncertainty that can have a **negative or positive** effect on meeting project objectives — security-side risk assessment in [[COMP504 Networks/Notes/Ch11 - Network Security|COMP504 Ch11]]
- Negative risk management: avoid, lessen (mitigate), change (transfer), or accept
- Positive risk management = investing in opportunities; **expected value** decides positive vs negative

## The 7 risk processes
1. **Plan risk management** → outputs a risk management plan (methodology, roles, budget/schedule, **risk categories / RBS**, probability & impact definitions, tracking, escalation path, risk-to-issue handover)
2. **Identify risks** — techniques: brainstorming / think-smash, **Delphi technique**, interviews, **SWOT analysis**
3. **Qualitative analysis** — prioritise by probability × impact (**probability–impact matrix**), **top-ten risk item tracking**, watch list
4. **Quantitative analysis** — numeric: **decision trees & Expected Monetary Value (EMV)**, simulation, sensitivity analysis
5. **Plan risk responses** — contingency plans, fallback plans
6. **Implement risk responses** — owner assignment (**RACI**), weekly risk reviews, change control for baseline impacts
7. **Monitor risk** — reassessment, **risk audits**, reserve analysis, response-effectiveness checks

## Reserves
- **Contingency reserves** (for known risks, in the cost baseline) vs **management reserves** (unknown risks, management control)
- Project **EMV ≈ its contingency reserve**: positive EMV → enough money; negative → need more

## The risk register
- Contains: ID, rank, name, description, category, root cause, **triggers**, potential responses, **risk owner**, probability, impact, status
- Risk sources tied to knowledge areas (e.g. scope: poor definition; time: estimating errors, critical path errors; cost: estimating errors, inadequate contingency; quality: poor attitude; HR: poor conflict management; stakeholders: lack of consultation, poor sponsor engagement)
- Common categories: market, financial, technology, people, structure/process; **RBS** = hierarchy of risk categories

## Risk identification activities (course examples)
- Student Services App: classify risks by knowledge area (mobile dev inexperience, API access unapproved, new framework, developer unavailability, privacy/security not reviewed, scope creep)
- Healthcare booking system: client hasn't finalised payment requirements, payment provider dependency, split developer, client unavailability, no security testing, no backup approach
