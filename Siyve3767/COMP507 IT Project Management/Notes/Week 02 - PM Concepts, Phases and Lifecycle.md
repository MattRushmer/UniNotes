# Week 02 - PM Concepts, Phases and Lifecycle

> Auto-extracted from [[COMP507 IT Project Management/Lectures/Week 02 - PM Concepts, Phases and Lifecycle.pptx|Week 02 - PM Concepts, Phases and Lifecycle.pptx]]

## Why PM matters
- Worldwide IT spending ≈ USD 5.43T (2025, Gartner); ~88M PM-oriented roles needed by 2030 (PMI)
- IT projects have a poor success record: Standish CHAOS 2020 — 31% cancelled, 35% challenged (scope/time/cost overruns), 34% delivered successfully; only 2.5% of corporations consistently meet scope/time/cost targets (PWC)
- Four causes of failed projects (Vaipulu, 2026): lack of PM methodology · project complexity · project challenges · internal issues

## Principles of PM (PMBOK 8th ed flavour)
- Stewardship · Team · Stakeholders · Value · Systems thinking · Leadership · Tailoring · Quality · Complexity · Risk · Adaptability & resiliency · Change

## What is a project?
- **Project** = "a temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value" (PMBOK 8th ed) — ends when objectives reached or terminated
- Attributes: unique purpose · temporary · progressive elaboration · needs resources (human + physical) · primary customer/sponsor · involves uncertainty and complexity
- **Operations / business as usual (BaU)** sustains created value; IT projects often move to operations after delivery
- **Product** (tangible or intangible component creating stakeholder value) vs **service** (application component providing functionality) — PMBOK 8th ed

## Triple constraint (iron triangle)
- **Scope · Time · Cost** define project success; different stakeholder perspectives (sponsor/client, end-user, team) must agree on success criteria
- **Project management** = applying knowledge, skills, tools, techniques to meet requirements (PMI definition)

## PM methodology and lifecycle
- Methodology = processes, procedures, practices, phases, tasks, and roles; the task list is the **WBS** (work breakdown structure) / work items
- Project lifecycle phases: **planning → analysis → design → development → testing → deployment** (planning phase is PMI-based; later phases follow the domain method — agile or structured)
- **Traditional methods**: waterfall · spiral (risk-driven) · V-model
- **Iterative/agile methods** (manage & deliver in short cycles): Scrum (sprints, reviews, retrospectives) · XP · Lean
- Structured methods focus on *how to build the product*; agile methods focus on *how to manage and deliver the work*

## Stakeholders & PM tools
- Stakeholders: people involved in or affected by the project — sponsor/portfolio, program managers, product owners, customers/end users, project team, operations, suppliers, even opponents
- Tools: project charter · scope statement · WBS · Gantt charts · network diagrams · **critical path analysis** · critical chain scheduling · cost estimates · **ROI** · **Earned Value Management (EVM)**
- "Super tools" (high use, high impact): scheduling software, scope statements, requirements analyses, lessons-learned reports
